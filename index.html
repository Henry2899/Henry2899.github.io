I am an M.Sc. student in Applied Mathematics at the Universidad Nacional de Colombia, Bogotá, where I also earned my professional degree in Mathematics (Matemático, 2026). My thesis, supervised by Prof. Diego Gerardo Roldán, studies discrete optimization problems — starting with the Traveling Salesman Problem — and how they can be formulated for quantum computing.

Most of my work follows the same path: take a real phenomenon, write a mathematical model for it, solve it numerically, estimate its parameters from data, and then ask how much the model can actually be trusted. I have followed this path with epidemics, satellite orbits, power grids and laboratory assays, mostly in Python and Julia.

A model earns my trust when the mathematics, the algorithm and the data agree with each other.

Research

My M.Sc. thesis (in progress, 2026–) builds on Bochkarev et al., Quantum computing for discrete optimization: a highlight of three technologies (EJOR, 2025). The first stage is classical: I model the TSP with the Dantzig–Fulkerson–Johnson, Miller–Tucker–Zemlin and quadratic-assignment formulations, compare their size, and solve benchmark instances with Gurobi. The next stage reformulates the problem as a QUBO, the input format of quantum annealers and variational quantum algorithms.

  • Discrete optimization & operations research Integer programming formulations, TSP and routing, exact, approximation and heuristic methods.
  • Quantum optimization QUBO models, quantum annealing, variational algorithms.
  • Mathematical modelling & scientific computing Differential equation models, numerical integrators, stability, dynamical systems.
  • Parameter estimation & uncertainty Least squares, Bayesian inference and MCMC, Monte Carlo, bootstrap.

Experience

Student research assistant — HERMES 500 project
Universidad Nacional de Colombia · 2026 · Supervisor: Prof. Vladimir Angulo Castillo · toolkit (synthetic data)

Statistical analysis of antioxidant activity in plant extracts. I built a reproducible Python workflow for the DPPH assay, estimated IC50 values by ordinary and weighted least squares, quantified their uncertainty with the delta method, Monte Carlo and bootstrap, and designed a Mamdani fuzzy inference system that combines potency and experimental precision into a single ranking index. I also carried out a methodological audit of a fuzzy model proposed within the project and an ANOVA-based analysis of total phenolic content across five species.

Selected projects

Simulated annealing for the Traveling Salesman Problem
H. Barrera, L. Torres · Markov Chains course project, 2026 · repository

Simulated annealing for the TSP, studied as a Markov chain on the space of tours.

Siting distributed solar generation on an urban power grid
Exploratory thesis project, 2026 · repository

Groundwork for siting photovoltaic generation in the distribution grid of Yopal (Casanare): a Python package with 44 automated tests that builds the network as a graph (NetworkX), validates its data, and checks voltages (LinDistFlow), line loading, losses and radiality on a reproducible 322-node synthetic network. The mixed-integer siting model and the demand-forecasting stage were formulated but not implemented before the project was paused.

An HSIR model of dengue in Neiva, 2024
H. Barrera, M. Caldón, A. Puenayán · Numerical Analysis course (Prof. Juan Carlos Galvis), 2025 · repository

A compartmental model fitted to the 2024 outbreak by least squares and Bayesian inference (Turing.jl), with a comparison of five numerical integrators. I built the dataset from the weekly epidemiological bulletins of Huila: I wrote extraction scripts (Python, regular expressions, OCR) for a format that changed during the year, and completed the data by manual transcription where the automatic extraction was not reliable.

Orbital modelling of the Starlink-1106 satellite
S. Adame, H. Barrera, D. Gayón, A. González · Mathematical Modelling I course, 2025 · repository

Four models of increasing complexity — from Newtonian gravity to Earth oblateness, atmospheric drag, lunar and solar perturbations and relativistic corrections — fitted to real ephemeris data with Nelder–Mead and Metropolis–Hastings in Julia. The best model reduced the fitting residual by almost three orders of magnitude.
